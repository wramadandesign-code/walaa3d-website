**The cost of a 3D product animation is set by the amount of work your specific product and video need — not by a fixed price per second.** Two 30-second films can require completely different effort: one might be a single product turning in a clean studio, the other an exploded view of fifty components with a full social-media package.

This guide explains every factor that affects cost, how they interact, and how to brief a project so you receive an accurate quote quickly. If you are new to the topic, start with [what 3D product animation is](/blog/what-is-3d-product-animation/).

## Why there is no standard price list

A 3D product animation is custom production. Before any video exists, someone has to prepare the product model, recreate its materials, design lighting and an environment, plan camera moves, animate, render and edit. How long each step takes depends on your product and your goals.

That is why a professional animator asks questions before quoting. A number given without seeing the product or understanding the goal is either padded to cover unknowns or likely to change later.

## The six factors that drive the cost

### 1. Product complexity

The more parts, moving mechanisms and different materials a product has, the more preparation and animation time it needs. A smooth plastic device with one button is quick to set up. A robot with sensors, wheels, light strips, a touchscreen and a tray system takes much longer — every material and moving part has to look and behave correctly.

### 2. The state of your 3D model

This is often the factor brands underestimate.

- **Clean CAD file available:** the animator converts and prepares it, fixes surfaces and organizes parts for animation.
- **CAD file that is very heavy or messy:** extra cleanup is needed so it renders smoothly.
- **No CAD file:** the product has to be modeled from photos, drawings or a physical sample, which adds significant time.

If you can share CAD data in a common format such as STEP, IGES, SolidWorks, Rhino, OBJ or FBX, you will usually save time and budget.

### 3. Video length and number of shots

Length matters, but the **number of shots** matters more. Each shot needs its own camera move, lighting adjustments and render time. A 30-second film with three long, elegant camera moves can be faster to produce than a 15-second film with twelve quick cuts.

### 4. Effects and special sequences

Some sequences add substantial work:

- **Exploded views**, where every component separates and reassembles in a controlled order
- **X-ray or transparent passes** that reveal internal parts
- **Mechanism animation** showing every position and accessory of a technical product
- **Screen and UI animation** on displays and touchscreens
- **Motion graphics**, text callouts and logo animations

You can see several of these combined in the [autonomous cleaning robot](/work/autonomous-cleaning-robot-animation/) and the [smart sensor exploded view](/work/smart-sensor-exploded-view-animation/) projects.

### 5. Deliverables and formats

A single 16:9 master film is one deliverable. A launch package might include the master, a 9:16 version for Reels and TikTok, a 1:1 or 4:5 version for feeds, several short cut-downs for ads, and still renders for your product pages. Re-framing and re-editing for each format takes time — but far less if it is planned from the start, because shots can be composed to work in several aspect ratios.

### 6. Deadline

A realistic schedule lets the animator plan review rounds and rendering efficiently. A rush deadline can require extra production resources and leaves less room for refinement. If you have a fixed launch date, share it immediately so the schedule can be planned backwards from it.

## How the factors combine: three example scenarios

The following scenarios show how scope changes effort. They describe the work involved, not prices.

| Scenario | What it includes | Relative effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple product showcase | One product, clean CAD, studio setting, a few slow camera moves, one format | Lower |
| Feature film for a launch | Several features explained shot by shot, lifestyle or branded environment, two or three formats | Medium |
| Technical launch package | Complex product, exploded view or X-ray pass, mechanism shots, UI animation, master film plus social cut-downs and stills | Higher |

Most projects sit somewhere between these, which is why the brief is so important.

## What is usually included in a quote

When comparing quotes, check that they cover the same things:

- **Model preparation or modeling** from CAD, photos or drawings
- **Materials, lighting and environment** design
- **Concept** — a storyboard or style frames before animation starts
- **Animation and rendering** of the agreed shots
- **Editing and compositing**, including text and simple graphics
- **Revision rounds** at preview stage
- **Final exports** in every agreed format and resolution
- **Music or sound design**, if needed (sometimes quoted separately)

Ask how many revision rounds are included and what happens if the scope changes after approval. Clear answers prevent surprises.

## How to reduce cost without reducing quality

You can keep budgets under control with a few practical choices:

1. **Focus the story.** Three to five key features are more persuasive than twenty.
2. **Provide a clean CAD model** and accurate material references (colour codes, finish samples, photos).
3. **Decide on formats early** so shots can be composed for every aspect ratio at once.
4. **Consolidate feedback.** Collect comments from your team into one list per review round.
5. **Reuse the model.** Once your product exists in 3D, new videos, variants and stills cost much less than the first project. Read more about it in the [electric water heater](/work/electric-water-heater-product-visualization/) project, where one model serves both a product film and e-commerce images.
6. **Allow a realistic schedule** instead of a rush deadline.

## How to get an accurate quote

Send a short brief that answers these questions:

- What is the product, and do you have CAD files? In which format?
- What is the goal of the video — launch, explainer, ads, trade show, sales?
- Which features must be shown?
- How long should the main video be, and which formats do you need?
- What is your deadline?
- Do you have examples of animations you like?

With that information, an animator can estimate the work precisely and suggest options that fit your budget — for example, a shorter master film plus social cut-downs instead of a long single video.

## Conclusion

The price of 3D product animation reflects the work your product and goals require: complexity, model preparation, shots, effects, formats and timing. Understanding these factors helps you plan a realistic budget, compare quotes fairly and get more value from every frame — especially when one 3D model can keep producing content for years.
